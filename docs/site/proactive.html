Calendar offers

Twice a day the runtime checks the calendar for events too close to drive between, and offers help.

What counts as a conflict

  • Two events in a row, both with addresses, at different places.
  • No other event overlaps the time between them, such as an all-afternoon block.
  • The gap between them is shorter than the drive plus 10 minutes, and under 3 hours.
  • A day with no conflict makes no model call and no message.

When the offer appears

The outer model picks a time from saved memories, such as “I'm usually up by 6.” With nothing saved, the midnight check sends at 8 am and the noon check at 6 pm.

  • The runtime moves the time to no later than 45 minutes before the second event.
  • An offer that comes due during a reply waits until the reply finishes.
  • An offer still waiting when the second event starts is skipped.
  • In a conversation with no messages yet, the offer waits until Nori's first greeting is sent.
  • The offer lists the read-only task's times with the clock time it read the calendar. After a yes, the outer model is told to read the calendar again before it suggests a time.
  • The offer is a message in the conversation. Nothing is sent by email or push.

What the user can do

AnswerResult
Yes, lookSends “Yes, please look for another time.” as a user message. Any task starts the usual way, and calendar changes still need approval.
DismissStops the read-only task. The same two events, at the same times and places, never get another offer.
A typed replyThe outer loop answers it like any other message.

Moving either event makes a new conflict, which can get its own offer.

Records and failures
  • Each half-day check has one record, so a retry of a finished check does nothing. Up to 4 more checks a day can be started by hand for testing.
  • An offer's ID comes from both events and their times, so finding the same conflict twice makes one offer.
  • A failed calendar read ends the check with no offer and logs the error. The next check runs as usual.
  • Each read-only task's full 5¢ counts against the 20¢ before it starts.
